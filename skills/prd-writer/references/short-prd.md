# Short PRD (one page)

Purpose: get agreement on the problem and the shape of the solution before detail is written. It should be readable in two minutes. This draws on the common one-pager formats and on the pitch format from Shape Up (problem, appetite, solution, risks, no-gos).

## Template

```
# <Feature or product name>
Owner · Date · Status (draft / agreed / building)

## Problem
Who has it, in what situation, and what it costs them today. One short paragraph with the evidence.

## Why now
What makes this worth doing ahead of other work.

## Goal and success measure
The outcome for the user, and the one number that shows it worked, with a target.

## Appetite
How much time this is worth. Scope is cut to fit, not the other way round.

## Proposed solution
The idea in a few sentences, with a rough sketch or flow if it helps. Describe what the person can do, not how it is built.

## Out of scope
What this will not do, and why.

## Risks and open questions
What could make this fail, and what is still undecided.

## AI notes (only if a model is involved)
What the model does, how good it must be, what happens when it is wrong, and the cost of one use.
```

## Rules

- One page. If it runs over, the problem is not yet clear or the scope is too wide.
- No solution detail beyond what is needed to judge the idea.
- Every claim about users needs a source or the word "assumption".

## Variant: PR/FAQ

When the user wants to test the story before the plan, write a one-page press release from launch day (who it is for, the problem, what is new, a customer quote) followed by the hard questions a customer and an executive would ask, with answers. It anchors on why anyone would care; the PRD anchors on what gets built.
