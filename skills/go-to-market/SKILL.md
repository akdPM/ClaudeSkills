# Go-to-market

For a solo builder the first goal is a hundred people who want this, reached by hand, not a scalable marketing machine. Narrow and specific beats broad at this stage.

## The method

1. **Define who it is for, precisely.** A segment narrow enough that you could list where to find them. "Small businesses" is not a customer; "product managers in India with two to five years' experience who are job hunting" is. Expect this to shift after the first thirty users.
2. **Position it against what they would otherwise do.** Work through: the alternatives (including doing nothing and the manual workaround), what this has that they lack, the value that gives the user, who cares most about that value, and the category that makes it obvious what this is.
3. **Write the message.** One sentence saying who it is for, what it does and why it is better. Then three proof points. Test it with `outsider`.
4. **Choose channels by testing, not by habit.** List many possible channels, pick three that fit where the audience already spends time, run a small cheap test on each, then put effort into the one that works. If most early sign-ups come from one place, stay there before trying another.
5. **Plan the first hundred by hand:** direct messages, communities and forums where the problem is discussed, your own network, building in public, one useful piece of content.
6. **Set the launch.** A quiet beta first to fix what breaks, then a public launch with a date, an offer and a place.
7. **Decide what you will measure:** sign-ups by source, how many reach the first real result, and how many pay.

## For AI products

- Lead with the result, not the technology. "Case study drafted from your documents in a minute" beats "AI-powered".
- Show real output. A live example builds more trust than a claim.
- Say plainly what it does not do and how accuracy is protected; buyers have seen overclaiming.
- Free trials cost money per use. Decide the free allowance with `pricing-and-credits`.

## Ask before starting

The user decides when this skill runs. When a task fits, ask one short question first ("Shall I use the go-to-market skill for this?") and start only after a yes. If the user named the skill, that is the yes.

## Who this is for

An indie AI product builder who is also an AI product manager. Assume small teams, limited budget, and products where a model produces part of the output. Wherever the work touches a model, cover quality, cost per use, latency and what happens when the model is wrong.
